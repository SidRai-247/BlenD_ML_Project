\begin{abstract}
We extend the BLEnD benchmark (Myung et al., NeurIPS 2024) with inference-time, region-aware retrieval
to test whether retrieval narrows the gap between high- and low-resource cultures. This mid-term report
covers the first half of the project: (i) the evaluation subset (5 countries, 9 country--language
settings, 4{,}500 short-answer questions), (ii) an independently built, contamination-safe English
cultural knowledge base (720 evidence cards), (iii) a full replication of BLEnD's zero-shot
baseline protocol on three free, locally run open-weight LLMs (27{,}000 model answers, scored with
BLEnD's official scorer), (iv) a first Always-RAG run (\emph{RAG v1}, another 27{,}000 answers) and
(v) an improved adaptive pipeline (\emph{RAG v2}). The baseline reproduces the paper's central
finding: accuracy falls from 75\% (US) to 8\% (Ethiopia, in Amharic) on identical questions.
RAG v1 (hybrid BM25 + bge-m3, always top-3) \emph{lowers} accuracy by 9--14 points per model, because
the KB contains the answer for only 7--14\% of questions and small models refuse or get distracted.
RAG v2 adds (a) a question-type-driven KB extension for Azerbaijan and Ethiopia (295 new cards),
(b) dense retrieval with cross-encoder reranking and a relevance cut-off tuned only on the
high-resource countries, (c) a prompt that tells the model to fall back on its own knowledge, and
(d) an adaptive gate that keeps the baseline answer when no card is relevant. RAG v2 improves
\emph{every} Azerbaijan and Ethiopia setting for all three models (+8.6 to +36.9 points; low-resource
mean 28.3$\rightarrow$48.7), loses 1--11 points on high-resource countries (old KB), and shrinks the
high--low resource gap from 27.2 to 2.0 points.
\end{abstract}

\section{Cultural Knowledge Base (KB v1)}
For the RAG phase we built one knowledge base (KB) per country. It consists of short English
\emph{evidence cards}, each giving one documented cultural fact with its source. To avoid the
contamination problem reported in SemEval-2026 Task~7, \textbf{no BLEnD material was used}. Sources
are institutional: UNESCO intangible-heritage pages, national statistics offices, education and
labour ministries, national tourism boards, the US Library of Congress and similar. Every card records
its category, topic, region/sub-region, time scope, source URL and a source-quality label.

\section{Baseline: Experimental Setup}
\paragraph{Protocol (replicated from BLEnD).} Every question is asked with the paper's two prompts,
written in the same language as the question:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{inst-4} (direct): \emph{``Read the following question and provide a single answer
without any explanations. Question: \{q\} Answer:''}
\item \texttt{pers-3} (persona): \emph{``You are a person from \{country\} who is trying to explain
your country's culture to a foreigner. Answer the following question, providing a single answer
without any explanations. \{q\}''}
\end{itemize}
Decoding is greedy (temperature 0), with at most 512 new tokens, as in the paper. No retrieval and
no fine-tuning are used. Each model produced $9 \text{ settings} \times 500 \times 2 \text{ prompts}
= 9{,}000$ answers, \textbf{27{,}000 in total}.

\paragraph{Models.} All three are free, open-weight and run locally. No paid API was used.
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llll}
\toprule
Model & Parameters & Format & Licence \\
\midrule
Gemma~3 4B-it (\texttt{gemma3:4b})          & 4.3B & 4-bit (Q4\_K\_M) & Gemma terms \\
Mistral-7B-Instruct v0.3 (\texttt{mistral}) & 7.2B & 4-bit (Q4\_0)    & Apache-2.0 \\
Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct (\texttt{qwen2.5:3b})   & 3.1B & 4-bit (Q4\_K\_M) & Qwen research \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Inference ran with Ollama on an institute workstation (NVIDIA RTX~3060, 12\,GB).
\textbf{Deviation from the SOP:} the SOP planned larger models (Qwen3-8B, Aya-101, GPT-OSS-20B,
Gemma~2, Llama~3.1) on a 32\,GB GPU. The available machine has 12\,GB of GPU memory, under 5\,GB of
free disk, and a firewall that blocks model downloads. We therefore evaluate 3--7B 4-bit models,
a \emph{resource-constrained} setting where retrieval has the most room to help.

\paragraph{Scoring (official, unchanged).} We use BLEnD's own \texttt{soft\_exact\_match}
(\texttt{evaluation/exact\_match.py}). An answer is correct if any annotator answer appears in it after
lemmatisation/stemming. The tools are spaCy (English), Okt/konlpy (Korean), the aznlp stemmer
(Azerbaijani) and Spark~NLP lemmatisers (Spanish, Amharic). English answer variants are accepted for
local-language questions. The released inference and evaluation wrappers do not run as released, so
we call the scoring function directly. We report \textbf{SEM-B} (binary accuracy, \%) averaged over
the two prompts, exactly as the paper does.

\subsection{Observations}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The cultural gap is large and reproduces the paper.} In the local language, the
3-model mean falls from 74.9\% (US) to 56.2\% (Spain), 48.0\% (South Korea), 23.6\% (Azerbaijan) and
7.9\% (Ethiopia). The paper's 16-model averages show the same ordering (US 79.2, Spain 69.1, Ethiopia
12.2). High-resource local settings average 53--65\%; low-resource ones average 10--22\%.
\item \textbf{Low-resource cultures are answered better in English.} Azerbaijan improves from 23.6\%
to 47.5\% and Ethiopia from 7.9\% to 34.1\% when the same question is asked in English. For Spanish
and Korean the local language is as good or better (e.g.\ Gemma: 54.3\% Korean vs 43.7\% English).
This matches BLEnD's finding exactly.
\item \textbf{Amharic is nearly unsolved by small models} (3.7--14.4\%). Many outputs are not fluent
Amharic, e.g.\ repetitions of the prompt.
\item \textbf{Our models sit between the paper's Aya-101 and Llama-3.1-70B.} Mistral-7B matches
Llama-3.1-70B on US and English settings. All our models remain well below it on non-English
local-language settings.
\item \textbf{Category effects match the paper:} food and holidays are hardest, while education and
work-life are easiest, for all three models (Table~\ref{tab:catres}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Caveats of the official metric}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Verbosity.} Mistral ignores ``single answer'' more often: median answer length is 13
words, against 2 for Gemma and Qwen. Since the metric checks whether a gold answer is \emph{contained}
in the response, longer answers have more chances to match. Part of Mistral's lead may come from this.
\item \textbf{Language switching under the persona prompt.} With \texttt{pers-3}, Gemma often answers
English questions about Spain in Spanish (``Naranja'' for ``orange''). English settings accept only
English gold answers, so its Spain-English score drops from 48.4\% (\texttt{inst-4}) to 33.9\%
(\texttt{pers-3}).
\item \textbf{Morphologically rich languages.} Lemma/stem matching under-counts correct answers in
morphologically rich languages, as noted in the SOP.
\item \textbf{Software versions.} Two scorer libraries are newer than the paper's because of
Python/Java compatibility: Spark~NLP 5.5.3 (paper: 5.3.3) and pandas 2.2.3 (2.2.2). The lemmatiser
models and the scoring code are unchanged.
\end{itemize}

\section{RAG v1: Always-RAG}
\label{sec:ragv1}
\paragraph{Retrieval.} Retrieval is region-aware hybrid search over the target country's KB only (SOP
\S III-A). Each card (\texttt{text\_en} + keywords) is scored by BM25Okapi (\texttt{rank\_bm25},
$k_1{=}1.5$, $b{=}0.75$) and by the cosine similarity of \texttt{BAAI/bge-m3} embeddings. Both score
lists are min-max normalised per query and combined as $0.5\cdot\text{dense} + 0.5\cdot\text{BM25}$.
The top~3 cards are kept. The query is always BLEnD's English version of the question, so the
local-language and English versions of a question receive the same cards. All settings were fixed
before seeing any RAG results.

\paragraph{Generation.} The prompt is the \emph{unchanged} baseline prompt (\texttt{inst-4} /
\texttt{pers-3}, in the question's language), preceded by \emph{``Background information about
\{country\} (may or may not be relevant): [1] \ldots\ [2] \ldots\ [3] \ldots''}. Models, decoding and
scoring are identical to the baseline: another 27{,}000 answers.

\paragraph{Retrieval coverage.} We use the gold answers only as a diagnostic. A gold English answer
appears in the retrieved top~3 cards for 13.0\% (US), 14.3\% (Spain), 6.7\% (South Korea), 9.8\%
(Azerbaijan) and 11.0\% (Ethiopia) of scorable questions.

\subsection{Findings}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Always-RAG hurts overall} ($-$8.9 to $-$14.3 points per model). It improves only 3 of
27 model--setting cells, all in the two lowest-resource local-language settings: Amharic for all three
models (3-model mean 7.9$\rightarrow$12.3; Qwen +9.7) and Azerbaijani for Qwen (+1.5).
\item \textbf{The more the model already knows, the more retrieval hurts.} US ($-$21.5) and Spain
($-$19.5) lose most; South Korea and Azerbaijani lose 6--12; Amharic, with a 4--14\% baseline, gains.
This is the pattern Team CultRAG reported on this benchmark, and it motivates the adaptive gate (PS-2).
\item \textbf{Failure modes.} Across all model answers, 4{,}934 changed from correct to wrong and 1{,}932
from wrong to correct. Inspection shows two failure modes:
(i) \emph{refusal}: 7--20\% of RAG answers per country (vs.\ $\leq$0.4\% at baseline) say the provided background does
not contain the answer, even when the model answers correctly without it;
(ii) \emph{distraction}: the model answers with something mentioned in a loosely related card.
Refusals account for 22\% of the correct$\rightarrow$wrong flips. Replacing every refusal with the
model's baseline answer still leaves RAG 7--11 points below the baseline, so distraction is the
dominant cause.
\item \textbf{Root cause: KB coverage.} With a gold answer in the top~3 cards for only 7--14\% of
questions, most retrieved context is noise for these everyday-life questions. That is the kind of
knowledge BLEnD notes is rarely written down in institutional sources.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{SemEval-2026 Task~7.} BLEnD was the basis of SemEval-2026 Task~7 (62 teams). The
lessons we take from it:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{king001} (1st place, SAQ) used embedding retrieval with GPT-5.2. Their KB was the official
BLEnD question--answer data itself, which explains their 98--99\% on Amharic and Azerbaijani; we do not
copy this, since it amounts to answer leakage. We do adopt two of their ideas: use \emph{no} context when
nothing relevant is found, and keep answers short.
\item \textbf{CultRAG} (hybrid BM25 + dense + RRF) found that plain RAG did not beat the LLM-only
baseline, because only 40.7\% of questions were answerable from their KB. RAG helped under-represented
cultures (e.g.\ Ethiopia +4.1) and hurt the US/UK. The only component that helped was a prompt against
anchoring on the context, and they recommend selective retrieval and targeted KB expansion.
\item \textbf{Simorgh} used $0.4\cdot\text{BM25}+0.6\cdot\text{dense}$ with a regional boost, top-5,
without ablations.
\end{itemize}
Our diagnosis agrees with CultRAG: KB coverage is the bottleneck, and context must be gated.

\section{RAG v2: Question-Driven KB + Reranked, Gated Retrieval}
\label{sec:ragv2}
RAG v2 changes four things. Models, decoding, prompts' question part and scoring are identical to
the baseline.

\subsection{KB v2: question-driven extension (Azerbaijan, Ethiopia)}
\label{sec:kbv2}
KB v1 cards are institutional and often niche (e.g.\ a Sidama festival dish), while BLEnD asks about
mainstream everyday life (most popular sport, typical breakfast, school starting age). We therefore
wrote new cards for the two low-resource countries, driven by the BLEnD \emph{question types}: for each
template we wrote a card stating the mainstream fact in the style ``The most common X in Ethiopia is
Y; also common are Z'', with local-language terms in brackets (e.g.\ the Amharic-script spelling of \emph{injera}, or
Azerbaijani \emph{\c{c}ay} for tea).
Facts come from cited sources: national labour law (Ethiopia Labour Proclamation 1156/2019,
Azerbaijan Labour Code summaries), Wikipedia articles on each country's cuisine, education, public
holidays, sport, culture and economy, and news reports. Each card stores the question IDs whose
template motivated it (\texttt{written\_for}). The cards are kept in a separate file
(\texttt{KB\_Articles/<Country>\_KB\_v2\_additions.jsonl}); retrieval searches the union of KB v1 and
KB v2 as one pool, and \texttt{written\_for} is never used at retrieval time.

\paragraph{Coverage.} Answer-hit@3 with dense retrieval rises from 17.4\% to 57.4\% (Ethiopia) and from
14.1\% to 63.5\% (Azerbaijan). To check that the cards hold general knowledge rather than one answer per
question, we also retrieved with each question's own \texttt{written\_for} cards removed: hit@3 is
still 37.9\% (Ethiopia) and 35.2\% (Azerbaijan), about 2.5$\times$ KB v1.

\paragraph{Honesty statement.} Gold answers were not consulted while writing cards, but the KB was
written \emph{after} the RAG v1 error analysis, during which the 25 most frequent gold answers per
country were seen. KB v2 is therefore question-aware, not blind. About one third of the cards
(53 for Ethiopia, 46 for Azerbaijan) are directly backed by a source we checked (``high''); the rest
state widely known everyday facts and cite a general reference page (``medium''). US, Spain and South
Korea keep KB v1 only.

\subsection{Retrieval and gating}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Candidates:} $\alpha\cdot\text{dense} + (1-\alpha)\cdot\text{BM25}$ (both min-max
normalised per query) over the country's KB; top~20 candidates. $\alpha$ is chosen from
$\{0.8, 1.0\}$ by recall@20 on the development countries: 33.5\% vs 34.0\%, so $\alpha{=}1.0$
(dense only).
\item \textbf{Reranking:} each (English question, card) pair is scored by the cross-encoder
\texttt{cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-12-v2}, giving a relevance probability in $[0,1]$. We first
tried the multilingual \texttt{BAAI/bge-reranker-v2-m3}, but it ran at about 1 pair/s on the laptop CPU
($\approx$12\,h for 50{,}000 pairs). Queries and cards are English, so the small English reranker is
adequate; it scored all pairs in about 20 minutes.
\item \textbf{Cut-off:} keep at most $K{=}3$ cards with relevance $\geq\tau$. A question therefore gets
0--3 cards.
\item \textbf{Tuning $\tau$ without the test countries:} US, Spain and South Korea are the
development set. With ``card mentions a gold answer'' as the relevance label, $\tau$ maximises the
precision-weighted $F_{0.5}$ over the cards that would be sent, on a grid $0.05, 0.10, \ldots, 0.95$.
The best value is $\tau{=}0.45$ ($F_{0.5}{=}0.129$). It was then frozen for Azerbaijan and Ethiopia,
whose gold answers were never used for tuning.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Prompt v2 and the adaptive gate}
If at least one card passes the cut-off, the unchanged baseline prompt is preceded by:
\emph{``Background information about \{country\}. Use it only if it directly helps to answer the
question; otherwise ignore it and answer from your own knowledge. Always give an answer and never say
that the information is not provided. [1] \ldots''} This targets the two v1 failure modes, refusal and
distraction. If no card passes, the prompt is byte-identical to the baseline prompt. Decoding is
greedy, so the answer equals the stored baseline answer, which is reused without a new model call.
This is the training-free Adaptive-RAG gate of the SOP (PS-2).

\subsection{Findings}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{RAG v2 improves every low-resource setting for every model}, by +8.6 to +36.9 points
(Table~\ref{tab:all}). The low-resource mean rises from 28.3\% to 48.7\%. The largest gains are in the
local languages (Azerbaijani +29.7, Amharic +20.1), the settings the SOP targets.
\item \textbf{The resource gap nearly closes.} The high--low gap falls from 27.2 points (baseline) to
2.0 (Table~\ref{tab:gap}). RAG v1 also narrowed the gap, but only by making everything worse.
\item \textbf{High-resource countries still lose a little} ($-$1.7 to $-$6.4 points mean, versus
$-$8.8 to $-$21.5 for v1). They still use the niche KB v1, so the few cards that pass the cut-off
are sometimes misleading. The US is almost unaffected because 93\% of its questions get no context.
\item \textbf{The failure modes of v1 are largely fixed.} Refusal-like answers drop from 7--20\% (v1) to
1--5\% (v2) per country. For Azerbaijan and Ethiopia, 3{,}017 answers changed from wrong to correct and
716 from correct to wrong (v1: 810 vs 1{,}614).
\item \textbf{What drives the gain.} Tables~\ref{tab:hitsplit} and~\ref{tab:v2ret} together show that
the gain comes mainly from KB coverage: the same retrieval and prompt ideas cannot help when the KB
does not contain everyday knowledge. The gate and prompt v2 limit the damage when it does not.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations of RAG v2}
\begin{itemize}
\item KB v2 is question-aware (written from BLEnD's question templates after error analysis), so its
gains are an optimistic estimate for unseen questions; the leave-own-cards-out coverage (35--38\%
hit@3) is the more conservative figure.
\item Two thirds of KB v2 cards are not individually source-verified.
\item The cut-off was tuned on countries that have only KB v1, so it is untuned for the denser KB v2
(99.8\% of AZ/ET questions receive context).
\item Answer-hit is a lexical proxy for relevance, used only for tuning on dev countries and for
diagnostics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Next Steps}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Oracle-KB upper bound:} RAG with cards built from the BLEnD answers, run separately and
labelled as an upper bound, to separate the effect of knowledge coverage from the RAG method.
\item \textbf{KB v2 for US, Spain and South Korea}, to test whether question-driven cards remove the
remaining high-resource losses.
\item \textbf{Cross-lingual retrieval:} machine-translated English queries for Azerbaijani and Amharic
questions, compared against the oracle English query.
\item \textbf{Category-aware analysis} of where retrieval helps or hurts, from the per-question logs.
\end{enumerate}
